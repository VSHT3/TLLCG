## Introduction
We are presented with a “Keep It Real” Public Service Announcment (PSA) campaign by the New Zealand Department of International Affairs about online bullying.

IIn the image, a young girl stands outside the open front door, framed inside a smartphone lockscreen with a notification about online bullying across, warning that bullying can be a crime. 

The PSA hints that online harm can invade the home as powerfully as any physical intruder, making visible the emotional loneliness and vulnerability of young people. 
It also uses powerful visual and textual features to show how online bullying hurts children, and to convince adults that they must pay attention to this possible threat and protect them.
## Body paragraph 1 – Visual eldrtements
The visual elements of the PSA are carefully composed so that the girl and the intrusion of online bullying are the main focus.  The young girl stands right in the middle of the open front door, framed by the doorway and the railing behind her, which makes her look simultaneously small and visually stuck in between the inside and the outside. The whole doorway has been turned into a phone’s lockscreen screen with the time, date, and battery at the top, so portrays the girl “inside” the phone, not just outside the house, meanwhile a notification blocks her from actually entering it.  This suggests that what happens on the phone is just as real and serious as what happens in the physical world. The warm, yellow light and flowers inside the house make the home look safe and cosy, while the outside looks colder and darker, which hints that the danger is coming from the online world. Because we see the scene from inside the house, it feels like we are the parents opening the door, which shows that the ad is aimed mainly at adults and parents.

---

## Body paragraph 2 – Emotion and body language

The girl’s body language creates a strong emotional reaction. She stands very still with her shoulders slightly hunched and a phone held up to her ear, which makes her look unsure and alone. Her stillness and the empty street around her give a feeling of silence and isolation, as if nobody is helping her. This makes viewers feel sympathy and worry, because the girl could be any child they know. By showing her in this quiet, tense moment instead of showing the bullying directly, the ad focuses on how the victim feels inside, using emotion (pathos) to make the audience care about the issue.

---

## Body paragraph 3 – Text and call to action

The short message at the bottom works with the image to push the audience to act. It looks like a real phone notification and is labelled “ONLINE BULLYING,” which makes it feel urgent and important. The line “Bullying others is never okay, and can be a criminal offence” is direct and serious, reminding adults that this behaviour is not just mean but can break the law. Words like “never” and “criminal offence” appeal to the audience’s sense of right and wrong and to their duty to follow the law. Because adults are the ones who usually see and understand such warnings, the ad is quietly telling them that they must step in and keep children safe from online bullying.








## Introduction


The visual and textual elements in the image, produced as part of the Keep It Real Online campaign run by New Zealand’s Department of Internal Affairs, construct a powerful warning about the emotional impact of online bullying and emphasise the urgent need for adult awareness and protective action.

## Body paragraph 1 – Visual construction of the message

The visual elements of the PSA are carefully composed to dramatise the intrusion of the online world into the apparent safety of the family home. The girl is placed centrally in the doorway and framed by the vertical lines of the doorframe and the horizontal lines of the veranda rails, which visually trap her between inside and outside and establish her as the focal point of the image. The designer overlays a smartphone lock‑screen interface over the entire doorway—complete with time, date, signal and battery icons—so that the door becomes a metaphorical screen and the girl appears to exist inside the phone rather than in the physical world. This layering turns the phone into a threshold between online and offline spaces, implying that what happens on‑screen has real‑world emotional consequences for the child. Inside the house, warm yellow lighting, soft curtains and a vase of flowers create a cosy atmosphere, whereas the exterior is cool‑toned and dimly lit, making the outside world look unwelcoming and isolating; this contrast underlines the dissonance between the child’s unsafe digital environment and the parents’ assumption of domestic security. The composition positions viewers just inside the home, as if they were the adults answering the door, which indicates that the intended audience is parents and caregivers who may be unaware of the extent of online harm their children can face.
## Body paragraph 2 – Emotional impact and pathos

The girl’s body language and expression are crafted to evoke empathy and concern, strengthening the PSA’s emotional appeal. She stands motionless in the doorway, with her shoulders slightly hunched and one hand holding a phone to her ear, which suggests a mixture of hesitation and dependence on the device that connects her to the bullying. Her downcast posture and the fact that she is alone at twilight create a sense of vulnerability, prompting viewers to imagine the fear and isolation that a bullied child might feel even while technically “at home.” The stillness of her figure contrasts with the immediacy implied by the on‑screen notification, reinforcing the idea that the emotional damage of cyberbullying can silently paralyse its victims. By focusing on a solitary child rather than showing explicit abuse, the PSA uses pathos to invite viewers to fill in the emotional gaps, encouraging them to project their own children or younger relatives into the scene and thereby deepening their sense of responsibility. This appeal to empathy aligns with the wider campaign’s goal of raising awareness of online harms—such as bullying, grooming and harassment—and motivating adults to talk to children about these issues.[](https://www.keepitrealonline.govt.nz/youth/about-us)

## Body paragraph 3 – Textual elements and call to action

The textual overlay of a push‑notification reinforces the visuals and transforms the image from a depiction of harm into a subtle call to action. Styled like a real smartphone alert and labelled “Online bullying,” the message states that “Bullying others is never okay, and can be a criminal offence,” adopting the concise, authoritative tone associated with system notifications to lend urgency and legitimacy to the warning. By echoing New Zealand’s legal framework for harmful digital communications, which treats certain forms of online harassment as potentially criminal, the language appeals to adults’ sense of legal and moral responsibility. The notification format suggests that this is not just information but a prompt that demands immediate attention, mirroring the way parents’ phones constantly alert them to work or social updates yet may not alert them to their children’s online suffering. The phrasing implicitly targets responsible adults—“bullying others is never okay”—reminding them that they must model respectful behaviour and intervene when they see signs of harm, a message consistent with the Keep It Real Online campaign’s emphasis on giving parents tools to recognise and respond to online risks. Through this combination of realistic digital interface, legal language and moral appeal, the PSA urges viewers to move from passive awareness to active protection of “young Kiwis” facing online harassment.
